# The Indian Left Intellectual

## Preliminaries

First, who is an intellectual? An intellectual I hereby define as a person who disseminates processed information to an audience. Note that in this weak definition of the term, the word **processing** is the criteria, which means that the Intellectual understands some state of affairs, either through observation or existing research, uses tools of analysis and criticism, and makes it presentable and lucid to his target audience. Intellectuals differ from Academics in their audience, and the motives. An academic is supposed to present research to her peers and stays relatively out of public view. Also, the academic must have an objective argument even for subjective claims by method of proofs. The intellectual on the other hand provides us with commentary and opinions with objectivity only as a background. The two are not disjoint, and especially within the social sciences, the two roles usually intersect, with most intellectuals almost necessary from an academic background as a certificate of their credentials to form opinions.

Since I am in Academia, I will focus on this intersection set, which will automatically apply to non academic intellectuals as well. The first axiom I state is that most intellectuals in the social sciences are predominantly left oriented in our country, and well, most of other countries as well. The second axiom is that these left intellectuals are either active social activists, or passive social activists, where the latter cohort is through activism away from the field through social media, conferences and so on. The third and final axiom is to divide this entire set into three categories:- **The Army**, **The Brains**, **The Naive**.(a direct borrowing from Scruton’s - The Firebrands,The Frauds, The Fools). Finally, the Left here are those who identify both *economically* and *politically* with Left.

In the *Genealogy of Morals(1887)*, Friedrich Nietzsche produced a stunning account and perhaps the first of its kind treatise on the evolution of moral values over time. In it, he demonstrated how the notion of **good** and **evil** inverted from the Greco-Roman civilizations to the Christian ethics of his time. It is surprising that a work of such a nature is perhaps more relevant now than ever.

The **good** as embodied by the passionate *Dionysian* hero, who was beyond the confines and constraints of narrow societal values which separated the Lion among men from the rabble.

In contrast, when the Church replaced the Roman Empire as the *de facto* Ruler of Europe, it did so via rabble rousing, where the erstwhile *passionate creative spirit* became the new **evil** in an inversion of moral values.
