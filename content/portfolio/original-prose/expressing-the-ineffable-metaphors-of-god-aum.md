## Expressing the Ineffable Metaphors of God - Aum

>In the beginning, was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the word was God
>
>> - John 1:1

>The Dao that can be named is not the Eternal Dao. The Name that can be named is not the Eternal Name. Nameless, it is the source of Heaven and Earth. Named, it is all the manifestations. It is found within darkness. Darkness within darkness - The Source of all mysteries
>
>> - Chapter 1, Tao Te Ching by Laozi

For an outsider, Hinduism is a labyrinth of myths, motifs, and rituals. There are literally thousands of gods, and even in terms of the supreme deity, one can be a follower of either the Trinity, i.e., Vishnu, Brahma, and Shiva, Shakti i.e. the Supreme Goddess, and even the mortal incarnations of Vishnu, the avatars Lord Ram and Lord Krishna - considered to be supreme deities in their own right.

This is not an essay about any of them.

Hinduism is a vast system with multiple philosophies that can consistently be viewed as either gnostic, agnostic, and even atheistic interpretations. It is also consistent with pantheism, monotheism, and monism (monism and monotheism are different concepts).

The most important systems are dualist or non-dualist, the former being Samkhya Philosophy, and the latter, Advaita Vedanta.

However different the philosophies might be, to the extent of contradicting one another, except a few, almost all schools of thought agree on one aspect at least - **On the Vedas being the supreme authority and divinely revealed**.

Thus the Vedas and their subsets - The Upanishads constitute Shruti i.e. Divine Revelation and the rest (including the Bhagavad Gita, surprisingly to many) constitute Smriti (That which is remembered). The former is literally the word of God. Of course, Shruti is also a work of man as are the Smriti but what makes them divinely revealed is that the sages who wrote them could say had reached enlightenment, through meditation, etc., and the objectivity of experiences beyond the realm of the empirical sphere granted their status. Of what was this, the primary literature - formed the secondary literature.

**Aum - God in a word, almost**

If you were to ask me what is the meaning of Hinduism, the religious belief, what it all points to, what all the epics, temples, and everything else points towards, it is the one symbol, **Aum**.

There is an interesting story that Lord Ram and Hanuman (Mundaka Upanishad) and that the Mandukya Upanishad is the clearest and most direct fact statement of the Upanishads. It states it as clearly as it can be, and due to its brevity and density, it often takes people many years of study, as well as meditation, to decipher them in full. The Mandukya Upanishad is one of the shortest Upanishads. It consists of 12 verses. The Upanishad deals with the metaphor Aum.

Out of the 12 verses, one verse of 5 lines is the most important paragraph in all of Hinduism, for it contains the definition of the transcendent. Before you go all excited, let me say, Upanishads are a philosophical text despite being a religion. It defines the transcendent the only way words can do it justice. Through what it is not - i.e. a negative definition. For the very point of the entire system is the ineffability (as will be explained below)

Here things might get a bit abstract and philosophical - so use some imagery, but remember the image is just a metaphor for the ineffable
