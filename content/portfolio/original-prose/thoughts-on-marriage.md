# Thoughts on Marriage

Marriage is a cultural institution built upon the twin foundations of Biological instincts and a Civilization's demands on the species.

The reproductive age is limited. The window starts waning at around 35 years for women, after which it gets increasingly difficult to conceive. Humans like all replicating containers need to further their genes onto the next generation, for the survival of our race. I'm sure though that this post-apocalyptic truth is deeply hidden in our animal brain, and biologically the demands from the male is to spread his seed as much as he can, to as many as he can, and for the woman to select the fittest out of the male gene pool - The alpha of the group[^though alpha-ness has changed from the best hunter to the best earner but I digress]

Around this animal psychology, Human Culture has civilized the reproductive process into the institution of marriage. Monogamous relations between a man and a woman, binding by contract. This allows order by preventing the male from spreading his seed everywhere and bolt away from the tedious task of raising the lamb. For of course, a sapien infant is as useless as a blob of jelly in managing its own survival.

But this article is not about any of that. I am expressing my detached thoughts on marriage according to the zeitgeist of a semi-upper-middle-class, metropolitan raised man in the second decade of the twenty-first century.

In the current socioeconomic and cultural landscape I find myself in, the following characterize what comes with the institution of marriage in general, applicable to me and the majority of the readers of this article, though with gender effects and so on. That is, these axioms must apply objectively (not psychologically)

1. Marriage is no longer forced upon without one's consent.
2. Marriage is no longer associated with the admirable goal of continuing our species. Lineage, perhaps.
3. Child rearing has turned to be an economic liability. No longer are children a pair of extra hands for the fields. A child is now a massive investment.
4. Women are increasingly economically independent.

Basically. What I want to convey is the fact that I analyze marriage from a purely Psychological level, in a sense controlling for other factors, be it institutional, sociological, cultural and their respective impact on psychology. So towards that end, I would not be considering even empirical psychology but the deep psychology of marriage.
