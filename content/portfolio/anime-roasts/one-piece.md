Right, so there’s this cartoon about pirates. Except it’s not really about pirates. It’s about a boy who ate a cursed fruit and is now functionally a rubber hose with impulse control issues. His lifelong ambition is to find a treasure that some guy, who was very good at being a pirate, said was real right before he was publicly executed. A stellar retirement plan.

This treasure, the "One Piece," is supposedly the ultimate prize. After 1,100-plus episodes, you start to suspect the real treasure is the syndicated television revenue the producers have picked up along the way. The main character, whose name is Monkey, wants to be the Pirate King. This is a job title that seems to involve a lot of smiling, eating entire pigs whole, and having friends who do most of the difficult work, like navigating or reading.

Our hero's primary superpower is stretching. Which is great if you need to get the remote from the other side of the couch, but less so when your enemies can create earthquakes or turn into light. To compensate, he learns to punch really hard. Sometimes he punches so hard his arm catches on fire. Don't ask how. The physics of this world were clearly designed by a committee that had just finished a very long lunch.

The central conceit of the power system is the Devil Fruit. You eat one, you get a cool power. The downside is that you immediately lose the ability to swim. In a world that is ninety-nine percent ocean. This is presented as a fair and balanced trade-off. It’s like selling your skeleton for a lifetime supply of jellybeans. Sure, it's sweet now, but you'll miss the structural integrity.

The plot follows a simple, repeatable formula. Step one: land on an island. Step two: find a local princess who is crying about a bad man. Step three: our rubber protagonist gets angry, inflates a limb to the size of a small bus, and solves the island's entire socio-political crisis with a single punch. Step four: have a big party. Repeat 50 to 60 times. Each time, the backstory of the new friend you meet is sadder than the last. His village was burned down. Her mother was executed. His talking reindeer guide was ostracized for having a blue nose. It's an emotional trauma conga line.

Watching One Piece is not a hobby; it’s a long-term commitment, like a mortgage or a difficult marriage. You could have learned a language, mastered an instrument, or built a reasonably-sized shed in the time it takes to get through the Dressrosa arc. But you didn't. You chose to watch a man in a straw hat punch a fish. And for that, you have my deepest, most indifferent sympathies.
