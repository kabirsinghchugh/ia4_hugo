Right, so there's this anime called Overlord. It’s about a guy who gets trapped in his favourite video game, which is every lonely person's second most-frequented fantasy, right after "what if I had said something witty in that argument from 2014".

Our hero, let's call him Bone Daddy, is the last remaining member of his guild, a group of friends who all eventually discovered the revolutionary concept of "having a life". So he's just sitting there, in his guild's ridiculously opulent evil fortress, waiting for the servers to shut down and finally release him back into his presumably soul-crushing corporate existence.

But, plot twist, the server doesn't shut down. Instead, all the non-player characters, which his friends coded to be absurdly, comically sycophantic, suddenly develop what we'll generously call "personalities". And by personalities, I mean they are all pathologically obsessed with him. It's like your toaster suddenly declaring its undying love and fealty because you once cleaned out its crumb tray.

Bone Daddy, now a giant, all-powerful magic skeleton named Ainz Ooal Gown, has exactly one goal: don't let anyone figure out he's just a regular guy on the inside who spent most of his adult life selling spreadsheets and is now improvising his way through godhood. This leads to a fascinating gameplay loop where his followers will interpret his most mundane actions, like, say, breathing (if he had lungs), as a move in a 10,000-year plan that they are too feeble-minded to comprehend.

"Oh, great Ainz-sama, you looked to the left! Truly, your wisdom is boundless! You must be signaling your intent to conquer the western kingdoms through a strategic alliance with the Mole People!"

Meanwhile, Ainz is just thinking, "Oh, a bird. Neat."

His main crew, the Floor Guardians, are a collection of the most powerful beings in the world, each with the emotional stability of a hamster on espresso. There's Albedo, a succubus whose entire existence revolves around wanting to climb Mount Bone Daddy. Then there's Shalltear, a vampire who is also horny for the skeleton, creating a workplace HR situation that would make most companies liquidate on the spot. We have a bug samurai, a dark elf twink and his marginally more-clothed sister, and a giant golem in a suit. They all share one brain cell, and it's dedicated to worshipping the calcium-rich protagonist.

The entire plot is basically this: Ainz accidentally stumbles into a new country. He tries to quietly gather information, but his subordinates, in their infinite sycophancy, misinterpret his orders and end up committing several war crimes and/or acts of genocide. The local population, who were previously dealing with normal fantasy problems like goblin infestations and tax season, are now facing an undead god-king who can kill 70,000 people with a single spell he learned from a microtransaction.

Ainz then feels vaguely bad about it for about five seconds before shrugging his bony shoulders and accepting his new role as the world's most reluctant tyrant. He is the living embodiment of "failing upwards," except his failures result in the utter annihilation of entire civilizations.

So, if you enjoy watching a socially awkward skeleton accidentally conquer the world while being worshipped by a harem of violently devoted monster girls, all because he's too embarrassed to admit he has no idea what he's doing, then Overlord is the show for you. It's basically a power fantasy for anyone who's ever felt like they were just winging it, but with more skeletons and gratuitous violence. A truly inspiring tale.
