Right, so there's this show. The premise is that one day, a big green light decides it's had enough of your nonsense and turns everyone into garden ornaments. A perfectly reasonable response to the state of the world, frankly.

For the next three thousand, seven hundred years, humanity just sort of... stands there. Pondering the profound emptiness of being a statue, probably.

Then our hero wakes up. His name is Senku, and his main personality trait is knowing science. All of it. He also has hair that looks like a vegetable that's been electrocuted, which is how you know he's the protagonist. His internal monologue is basically a Wikipedia speedrun, and his first thought after millennia of being a rock isn't "what fresh hell is this?" but "Right then, time to invent capitalism again, starting with the chemistry."

He immediately finds his friend, a large, emotionally compromised young man whose primary skill is shouting very loudly. His job is to do the lifting, because our leek-haired protagonist has the physical constitution of a wet noodle. Together, they form a crack team to restart civilization. One guy to remember how to make soap, and another guy to carry the soap. The pillars of society.

Their plan is immediately complicated by the arrival of the antagonist, a man who is essentially a sentient pectoral muscle with a messiah complex. He decides that the old world was bad because of, you know, adults and property taxes. His solution is to smash any adult statues he finds, creating a utopia populated exclusively by strong teenagers. It's a political ideology ripped straight from a gym changing room.

So the conflict is basically Brains vs. Brawn. Except Brains has the uncanny ability to invent everything from gunpowder to a functioning mobile phone using sticks, some questionable liquids, and the power of a conveniently timed thunderstorm. You need sulphuric acid? Don't worry, just wander into this cave and you'll find a lake of the stuff, perfectly safe. Need tungsten? The strongest metal known to man? Just ask one of the villagers, they probably use it as a paperweight.

The show is basically a speedrun of the technology tree from a video game, played by a guy who has all the cheat codes. Every problem is solved by Senku staring intently at a rock and saying "10 billion percent, we're going to make a gramophone." And then they do. Because science.

It's the ultimate fantasy for anyone who thinks they could totally survive the apocalypse because they once watched a documentary about how glass is made. Spoiler: you wouldn't. You'd be the first one to get eaten by a boar. But it's nice to pretend that all you need to fix the end of the world is a good memory and some very, very patient friends who are happy to do manual labour for 18 hours a day in exchange for a bowl of ramen. Exhilarating.
