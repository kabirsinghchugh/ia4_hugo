Ah, High school! Where your primary goal is to make it through four years without anyone realising you still don't know how to tie a proper knot.

And then there's the high school in Classroom of the Elite. A school which requires possession of a tactical mind of a chess grandmaster, a doctorate in the principles of Machiavelli, and a belief that the current Japanese education system isn't stressful enough - just to survive the next few months. 

## Part 1: The School

Welcome to the Tokyo Metropolitan Advanced Nurturing High School, a name that sounds like it was generated by ChatGPT (the cheaper one)  

The sales pitch is simple enough: graduate from here, and you can have any job you want. The catch? the school is a libertarian nightmare run by teenagers.

You're given 100,000 points a month, which is basically money, but the school can yoink it all away if someone in your class breathes too loudly during a lecture. 

Students are sorted into four classes, A through D. Class A is for the geniuses and future prime ministers. 

Class D is for the … "defects". This is the educational equivalent of sorting your rubbish into "recycling" and "bin fire".

Our story, naturally, focuses on the bin fire.

## Part 2: The Protagonist - ChadGPT

Meet Kiyotaka Ayanokouji. His personality is a flat line. His emotional range is somewhere between a brick and a slightly different brick. He claims his only goal is to live a "*normal, peaceful high school life*", which is the biggest lie since "I have read and agree to the Terms and Conditions".

Ayanokouji is what you'd get if you gave a supercomputer a school uniform. He spends 90% of his time staring out a window, looking as bored as a man who's already calculated every possible outcome of the universe and is just waiting for the heat death part.

His core philosophy is that **everyone is a tool**, literally. 

Some friends are screwdrivers, some are specialised, high-maintenance power drills. Classmates are an assortment of nuts and bolts you use to fix a problem. He is the  handyman, and his project is to orchestrate a slightly better class ranking while expending the least amount of energy possible. 

## Part 3: The Toolshed

 * Horikita: She's the "main girl" who sits next to the protagonist. Her defining character trait is being incredibly unpleasant to everyone because she believes friendship is an inefficient use of resources. She thinks she's playing 4D chess, but she's actually just knocking over the checkers board while Ayanokouji solves a Rubik's Cube behind her back. Her entire motivation revolves around her brother, who has successfully forgotten she exists. it’s quite hilarious tbh. 

 * Kushida: The human embodiment of Instagram. On the surface, she's friendly, popular, and wants to be friends with everyone. On the inside, she's a seething cauldron of rage and spite who secretly wants to watch the world burn. Her secret is so monumentally important that she's willing to sabotage her entire class just so nobody finds out she was a bit of a git in middle school. *Relatable*.

 * Kei: Her character arc is going from a traumatised victim to the protagonist's main tool and designated girlfriend. This is framed as progress. She is the first tool to realise she is a tool and be sort of okay with it, which in this universe is peak self-actualisation.
 * Everyone Else: Then there’s this loud one who's good at basketball, the handsome one who is the "leader". The nerdy-bookish-spectacled one and others. All we have to know is that they are all background characters in the Ayanokouji Show, occasionally given a line of dialogue so he has something to react to with his signature blank stare.

## Part 4: The Plot, or "The Special Exams".

The plot is just a series of "Special Exams" which are essentially episodes of Survivor but with more brooding and internal monologues.

There's the deserted island exam, where teenagers have to budget points and survive in the wild.

*Ayanokouji wins by doing basically nothing until the last five minutes*.

There's the cruise ship exam, a labyrinthine group test that's so confusing the characters spend half the time explaining the rules to each other. 

*Ayanokouji wins by doing basically nothing until the last five minutes*.

There's the zodiac exam, and the paper shuffle exam, and probably an exam where they have to make a nice sandwich for all I know. What matters is that the pattern is consistent. 

- A problem appears. Everyone panics.
- Horikita makes a suboptimal plan.
- Ayanokouji pulls a few invisible strings, utters a vaguely philosophical quote about the nature of man, and ensures Class D doesn't completely fail. He then takes zero credit, because maintaining his cover as "aggressively mediocre boy" is the most important mission of all.

Classroom of the Elite is a show about a boy genius who could probably cure cancer or achieve world peace, but instead dedicates his immense intellect to manipulating high school exams for marginal gains. It's a power fantasy for people who think the smartest person in the room is always the quietest one, and not just the one who's trying to figure out if they left the oven on at home.

And we watch it, because deep down, we all want to believe that our social awkwardness isn't a flaw, but a sign of our hidden, transcendent genius. It isn't, but it's a nice thought.
