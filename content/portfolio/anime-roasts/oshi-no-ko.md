Right, so there's this cartoon everyone's been talking about, "Oshi no Ko". It's a gripping tale about what happens when you take the reincarnation trope, the idol industry, a murder mystery, and a revenge plot, and throw them all into a blender without the lid on.

It all starts with a doctor, who's a massive fan of an idol named Ai. One day, she shows up at his rural hospital, pregnant. Before he can even process this breach of medical ethics, he gets pushed off a cliff by a disgruntled fan and is immediately reborn as her son, Aqua. His twin sister, Ruby, is also a reincarnated fan. So now we have two fully conscious adults trapped in the bodies of infants, which is not at all horrifying.

Their mother, the super-famous idol, gets tragically un-alived in the first episode, because of course she does. This sends Aqua on a decade-long quest for revenge against his anonymous father, a man he's never met but is convinced is the ultimate evil. His plan? To become a famous actor and somehow, through the magic of networking, find and confront his dad. It's a foolproof strategy, with absolutely no flaws.

Meanwhile, Ruby, his sister from another life, decides to follow in their mother's footsteps and become an idol. She's driven by a pure-hearted desire to shine on stage, completely oblivious to the fact that her brother is treating the entire entertainment industry like a giant game of Clue.

The show then spends the next several hours pretending to be a deep, insightful critique of the idol industry. It tells us that being famous is hard, that fans can be weird, and that social media is a bit of a nightmare. Groundbreaking stuff, really.

Along the way, Aqua assembles a harem of emotionally vulnerable teenage girls, including a child-prodigy-turned-washed-up-actress and a girl who's so good at method acting she basically becomes a different person. He manipulates them all for his grand, convoluted revenge scheme, because he's a brooding, dark, and mysterious protagonist, and that's what brooding, dark, and mysterious protagonists do.

So, if you're looking for a show that combines the emotional depth of a soap opera with the plot coherence of a fever dream, and you have a burning desire to watch a reincarnated doctor solve a cold case by starring in a reality dating show, then "Oshi no Ko" might just be the show for you. Or, you could just watch a documentary about the actual entertainment industry. It's probably less confusing.
