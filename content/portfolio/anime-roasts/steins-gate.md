Right. Let’s talk about Steins;Gate. It’s a story about a man who defeats a global conspiracy by shouting at a microwave.

First, you have our hero. He calls himself a mad scientist, which is a fun way of saying he’s a university student with too much free time and a social circle you can count on one hand, provided that hand has been in an industrial accident. His main personality trait is wearing a lab coat over a t-shirt, which isn’t a personality, it’s a cry for help. He spends most of his day talking into a switched-off phone to an imaginary organisation. This is treated as charmingly eccentric, when in reality it’s grounds for a wellness check.

His secret laboratory is a flat above a television repair shop. It is less ‘laboratory’ and more ‘fire hazard with a sofa’. Here, with his crack team, he invents things. The team consists of:

 * A pervert who is very good at computers. His job is to provide technical support and occasionally remind you that the show is, in fact, Japanese.
 * A girl whose entire function is to say “Tuturu~” and be so profoundly innocent that her continued existence is a statistical anomaly. She is the human equivalent of a wind chime.

Their greatest invention, the pinnacle of their scientific genius, is the Phone Microwave. You put a banana in it, you send a text message, and the banana arrives on the counter as a gelatinous green blob. Congratulations, you’ve discovered a very inefficient way to ruin fruit.

Except this isn’t just a fruit-ruiner. It’s a time machine. Because of course it is. Sending a text message while the microwave is on sends the message into the past. This is what happens when you let an arts student name a scientific principle.

For the first twelve episodes, they use this universe-shattering power to do things like win the lottery for a few quid and check what it’s like to have been born a girl for a week. The stakes are lower than a conversation with your cat. It’s a slice-of-life anime where one of the slices is occasionally a sad, green banana.

Then, the show remembers it’s supposed to have a plot. A sinister organisation named SERN, who are basically the EU but with more particle accelerators and less bureaucracy, wants the time machine. Things get dark.

And by "dark," I mean the wind-chime girl starts dying. A lot. She gets shot. She gets hit by a train. She has a heart attack. She trips on a banana peel, probably. It’s a cosmic joke where the punchline is always her dying in a new, creative way. Our hero, who until now has just been yelling nonsense, suddenly has to get serious.

His grand mission becomes undoing all the tiny, selfish text messages he and his friends sent to the past. This is the main thrust of the show: a man frantically trying to put the timeline back to its original, slightly disappointing state. It’s like trying to un-send a drunk text to your ex, but with more tears and a higher body count.

Every time he fixes one thing, he has to watch his friends’ brief moments of happiness evaporate. It’s a tour of suffering where he is both the guide and the only visitor.

The solution, in the end, is not to be smarter or stronger. The solution is to trick yourself. You have to go back in time, watch your actual scientist friend get stabbed, then travel forward, build a real time machine, go back again, and throw a bucket of fake blood everywhere to convince your past self that she died, so that the universe just sort of shrugs and moves on. The climax of the show is successful gaslighting.

So, to recap: a man LARPing as a scientist accidentally invents time travel with a kitchen appliance, breaks reality trying to win the lottery, spends weeks watching his friend die on a loop, and fixes it all by committing insurance fraud against the space-time continuum.

It’s the choice of Steins Gate. Whatever that is. Don’t think about it too hard. The banana didn't.
