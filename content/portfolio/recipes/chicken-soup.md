## Comforting Chicken Soup: a Flavor-first Approach

This recipe prioritizes deep flavor and richness for a truly comforting and restorative chicken soup.

### Why This Recipe Works

1. **Whole Chicken for Rich Broth:** Using a whole chicken is crucial. The bones contribute collagen for body and a silky mouthfeel, while the skin and fat render into flavorful _schmaltz_.
2. **Aromatic Foundation:** Sautéing garlic, ginger, and onion first builds a complex, savory base through the Maillard reaction. Skipping this step results in a flatter-tasting soup.
3. **Efficient Pressure Cooking:** Pressure cooking extracts maximum flavor and breaks down connective tissues quickly, achieving in 30–40 minutes what would take hours on a stovetop.
4. **"Unstrained" for Depth:** Not straining the soup and returning broken-up chicken (bones and all) is key to its rich character. Suspended particles of protein, fat, and marrow provide substantial nourishment and flavor — a rustic comfort distinct from a clear consommé.
5. **Thoughtful Finishing Touches:**
    - **Umami:** Soy or fish sauce adds a deep, savory note that complements the chicken.
    - **Acidity:** A squeeze of lemon or dash of vinegar brightens flavors and cuts through richness, preventing the soup from feeling heavy.
    - **Fresh Aromatics:** Toasted sesame oil and fresh herbs (coriander/spring onions) are added at the end to preserve their volatile aromas, providing a fresh contrast to the slow-cooked broth.

### Ingredients

- 1 whole chicken (approx. 1.2–1.5 kg)
- 10 large garlic cloves
- 1 thick, thumb-sized piece of ginger (approx. 2 inches)
- 1 medium onion
- 1 tablespoon neutral oil (e.g., sunflower, rapeseed) or butter
- 8 black peppercorns
- 1½ teaspoons salt (for initial seasoning)
- 2½ litres water (or enough to almost submerge the chicken)

**Finishing touches (optional but recommended):**

- 1 teaspoon light soy sauce (can be added with initial seasonings)
- A squeeze of fresh lemon juice or dash of vinegar
- A few drops of toasted sesame oil
- A handful of finely chopped fresh coriander or spring onions

### Method

**Step 1: Mise en Place**
- Peel and roughly chop the onion.
- Peel and lightly smash the garlic cloves.
- Peel and slice the ginger into thick coins.
- Gather your chicken, salt, peppercorns, and oil.

**Step 2: Build the Flavor Base**
- Place your pressure cooker pot on the stove or plug in your Instant Pot.
- Select the Sauté function and allow it to heat for 1 minute.
- Add the oil or butter. Once shimmering, add chopped onion, smashed garlic, and sliced ginger.
- Sauté, stirring occasionally, for 3–4 minutes until softened and lightly golden.

**Step 3: Combine and Seal**
- Turn off the Sauté function.
- Carefully place the whole chicken into the pot, on top of the aromatics.
- Add black peppercorns, salt, and the optional soy sauce.
- Pour in the water — it should nearly cover the chicken without exceeding your cooker's max fill line.
- Secure the lid.

**Step 4: Pressure Cook**
- **Instant Pot:** Set steam valve to Sealing. Select Manual or Pressure Cook for 25 minutes on High Pressure.
- **Stovetop pressure cooker:** Place on medium-high heat. Once at full pressure (first whistle), reduce heat to medium-low and cook for approximately 20 minutes (or 3–4 whistles).
- After cooking, turn off the heat and allow the pressure to release naturally (15–20 minutes). Do not force release — natural release gives more tender meat.

**Step 5: Shred the Chicken**
- Once pressure is fully released, carefully open the lid.
- The chicken will be very tender. Use tongs to lift it onto a plate and let it cool for about 10 minutes.
- Pull the meat off the bones into bite-sized pieces using two forks.
- Return all the shredded meat — and some smaller bones for maximum flavor — back into the broth.

**Step 6: Final Seasoning and Simmer**
- Bring the soup back to a gentle simmer on low heat. Let it bubble for 5 minutes to meld flavors.
- Taste and adjust: add salt pinch by pinch, a small squeeze of lemon if it tastes flat, or more soy sauce if you want more depth.

**Step 7: Serve**
- Ladle into bowls. Just before serving, drizzle each bowl with a few drops of toasted sesame oil and scatter fresh coriander or spring onions on top.
